---
title: How PnL is calculated
description: The average-cost method behind your profit and loss, with a worked example.
---

PnL means profit and loss. %APP_NAME% uses the average-cost method: every token you hold has one
average price, and each sale is measured against it.

## The rules

- **Buying** adds the tokens and everything you paid for them, fees included. Your average cost is
  the total you paid divided by the tokens you hold.
- **Selling** gives a realized result: what the sale brought in, after the fee, minus the average
  cost of the tokens you sold. The tokens you keep stay at the same average cost.
- **Tokens you receive** from another wallet count at their price when they arrive. Receiving
  tokens is never a gain or a loss by itself.
- **Tokens you send** to another wallet take their share of the cost with them, and realize
  nothing.
- **Unrealized** is what the tokens you still hold are worth at today's price, minus what they
  cost.

## A worked example

| Step                  | Tokens held | Total cost | Average cost | Realized |
| --------------------- | ----------: | ---------: | -----------: | -------: |
| Buy 10 JUP for $5.00  |          10 |      $5.00 |        $0.50 |    $0.00 |
| Buy 10 more for $7.00 |          20 |     $12.00 |        $0.60 |    $0.00 |
| Sell 5 for $4.00      |          15 |      $9.00 |        $0.60 |   +$1.00 |

The 5 tokens sold cost $3.00 at the average of $0.60, and brought in $4.00: a realized result of
+$1.00. At a price of $0.70, the 15 tokens still held are worth $10.50 against a cost of $9.00: an
unrealized result of +$1.50.

## Exact to the millionth

Dollars are counted in millionths, and token amounts in each token's smallest unit, so nothing
drifts after many trades. When a split can't be exact, it's rounded to the nearest millionth of a
dollar, and a half rounds up.

## The order of events

How many tokens you hold comes from the blockchain itself. Your cost and results come from your
trades and transfers, applied in the order they happened on-chain. A transaction that shows up late
gives the same result as if it had arrived on time.

The method is open source, in
[`packages/pnl`](https://github.com/aeron-playground/Opentrail/tree/main/packages/pnl), under the
MIT license, with a test for this example.
