---
title: "ADR 0002: Wallets whose keys we never hold"
description: People sign in with Privy and get an embedded Solana wallet. The private key never reaches our servers.
---

## Status

Accepted on 2026-09-24.

## Context

An app that holds its users' keys is a custodian: one breach can empty every account, and the
legal duties are heavy. At the same time, people expect to sign in with email or a social account,
not to manage a seed phrase from the first minute.

## Decision

Sign-in and wallets come from Privy. Each person gets an embedded Solana wallet, and transactions
are signed on their own device. The private key is never sent to our servers, stored or logged.
The same account and wallet work on the web and in the mobile app.

The server learns a person's wallet address from Privy, never from what the client sends.

## Consequences

- We can't move anyone's funds, and a breach of our servers exposes no keys.
- Anyone can export their key through Privy and use the wallet in another app.
- Sign-in depends on Privy. If that stops fitting the project, another wallet provider can take
  its place behind the same flow.
- No code may ever ask for, store or log a private key or a seed phrase.
